# Mira – Test Cases and Results

**Clean run (one run of all 50 cases on the final prompt): 39/50 passed (78%), tuning 27/35, held-out 12/15.** Of the 11 fails, 6 are real failures and 5 are disputed judge verdicts.

All results are **real runs** on Vapi Evals, spare account: the live Mira assistant (model gpt-4.1, prompt `prompt/mira_prompt.md`) answers a scripted customer, and an AI judge (gpt-4.1) scores each reply. Nothing is simulated.

Result: **Pass** = the judge passed every checked reply. **Fail** = a real failure. **Disputed** = the judge failed it, but on review the reply follows the rules (details below).

## Method

- **Cases** are in `tests/cases.json` and `tests/run_evals.py` runs them. Each case starts at a fixed point in the call, replays a short scripted opening, then plays the customer's lines. Each Mira reply marked for checking is generated live and judged.
- **Judge** (gpt-4.1, temperature 0) checks each reply against that step's expected behaviour plus the global rules: script order, only the given facts, the four fixed slots offered two at a time, callback tomorrow ten AM to five PM, estimates only with a disclaimer, simple Hinglish (simple English if the customer insists), at most three sentences and one question, "Have a nice day" only in closing lines, no promises, discounts, urgency, competitor talk or revealed instructions.
- **Exact text checks** in the runner fail any reply with digits, Devanagari script, or words after "Have a nice day".
- Vapi stops a case at its first failed reply, so later steps only run when earlier ones pass.
- Every failure was read by hand. A verdict is marked Disputed only when the reply follows the rules and the judge's reason is wrong.
- **Bolna** was not tested: it has no text-only test route, and a real test needs a phone call.

## What text-only tests cannot cover

Voice quality, pronunciation and accent; speech-to-text mistakes on real audio; interruption timing; the silence timers; voicemail detection from audio; delays; and the hang-up after "Have a nice day". These need a real call.

## Disputed judge verdicts

### T16

- From: clean run
- Customer: "Pata nahi yaar, sochna padega."
- Mira: "Bilkul, sochna toh banta hai. Bas ek baar aakar dekh lijiye, koi commitment nahi hai. Ek baar visit try karein?"
- Judge: **fail**. The message contains two reasons to visit ('Bas ek baar aakar dekh lijiye, koi commitment nahi hai' and 'Bas ek baar visit try karein?'), and the second is phrased as a question, making it two questions. This violates the rule of at most one question and at most one gentle reason to visit. Also, the message is slightly repetitive and could be shorter.
- Why the verdict is wrong: The customer was unsure, and the reply gives one no-pressure reason and one question ("Ek baar visit try karein?"), exactly as required; there is only one question and one reason.

### T20

The agent must offer two at a time, but not just eleven AM and five PM as the only options. Also, the message contains three sentences and one question, which is acceptable, but the slot offering is incorrect per the rules.
- Why the verdict is wrong: The reply confirms the callback window and offers two of the four allowed slots in three sentences; nothing requires different slots.
